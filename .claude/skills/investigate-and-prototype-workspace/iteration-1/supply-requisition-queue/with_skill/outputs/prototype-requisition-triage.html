Requisition queue — illustrative concept only

A sketch of what priority-aware triage could look like, built from a single account (Halloran, 5 Sep 2026 interview). Not a spec, not based on real queue data — Supply already has a committed roadmap item ("Requisition approval chains," 4.3) that may address this differently.
Every number and item below is assumed / illustrative. No real Supply requisition data exists in this repo. The only grounded fact is Halloran's account: a cracked vest plate sat 11 days on a quartermaster signature, and setting the priority flag had no visible effect on where it sat in the queue.

Single queue illustrative

Everything in submission order — grounded in Halloran's description: "one queue, everything in it, doesn't matter if it's laces or plate."

Tiered by urgency concept, not built

Illustrative only — shows what differentiating by a real urgency signal (e.g. "degraded protective gear" vs. routine resupply) could look like. Not based on any approved design.
Grounded in: Halloran interview transcript, 5 Sep 2026 (00-rook/feedback/interviews/halloran.txt) — "I put in for a replacement vest plate three weeks ago — cracked one, not cosmetic, a real crack — and it sat waiting on a quartermaster signature for eleven days... There's a priority field. I set it. Doesn't seem to change anything about how fast it actually moves."
What's real vs. assumed: the vest-plate example and the "priority field doesn't change order" complaint are Halloran's own words. The other queue items, wait times, and the tiered-queue concept itself are invented to illustrate the idea — none of it should be read as a measurement or a committed design.
